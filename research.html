Applied AI Research · Information Retrieval · Evidence

Investigating what makes retrieved information
sufficient to answer a question.

ParancU Lab explores the limits of semantic document retrieval and investigates methods for identifying factual evidence within source material.

The work combines prototype development, controlled experiments and evaluation of both successful and unsuccessful approaches.

Relevant does not necessarily mean sufficient.

Semantic similarity is not answer containment. A passage can relate closely to a question without containing the facts needed to answer it. Embedding-based retrieval can find factual answers; similarity alone does not establish sufficiency.

Illustrative example, not an experimental result: a manual says, “Do not operate the pump above 60°C.” A question about whether operation at 80°C is permitted concerns the same topic, but an answer must preserve the prohibition and temperature condition.

Missing qualifications, negation, incorrect factual relationships and incomplete evidence can make relevant passages misleading. Sometimes the answer is absent from the available documents. Recognizing these boundaries is an open research problem.

ParancU 2.0 — Factual retrieval

ParancU Decision Lab — Evidence assessment

Investigates evidence sufficiency, complementary passages, missing-information diagnosis, verification boundaries and targeted retrieval recovery.

Evaluation

Evaluate the evidence,
including failure cases.

Experiments examine whether:

  • Relevant passages are retrieved and contain the required facts.
  • Multiple passages jointly provide sufficient evidence.
  • Important conditions and negations survive processing.
  • Insufficient evidence is recognized.
  • Conclusions remain traceable to the original source.

Retrieval rankings and evidence-containment measures are distinct from answer correctness. High similarity or a successful isolated benchmark does not establish general factual reliability.

Findings & limitations

What the experiments have shown,
and what remains unresolved.

Research snapshot · 20 October 2026

ParancU v1.1 provided a working embedding-based retrieval baseline. Implemented newer components include source-linked sentence representations, multiple-question retrieval and experimental evidence-selection logic.

Question-based representations and alternative matching strategies have been investigated. Some small controlled comparisons produced encouraging results, but learned-matching experiments failed their success criteria and a source-binding follow-up exposed attribution failures.

Decision Lab has tested sufficiency and selection components in synthetic scenarios and selected real-document evaluations. Results include successful judgments, false sufficiency judgments and rejected sufficient evidence. Synthetic outcomes are not real-document performance estimates.

Software tests and model-generated judgments do not constitute independent validation of factual correctness. General reliability and generalization beyond the evaluated material remain unestablished.

Open questions

Research remains open.

  • How can retrieval distinguish topical similarity from factual answerability?
  • How should conditions, negations and factual relationships be represented?
  • When are multiple passages sufficient, and how should missing information be recognized?
  • Which approaches generalize beyond controlled datasets?
  • How can future architectures preserve privacy and source traceability?

Cloud processing and API access are possible development directions, not established services or a committed architecture. Document confidentiality remains a design requirement; processing, access and retention arrangements require definition and validation.
